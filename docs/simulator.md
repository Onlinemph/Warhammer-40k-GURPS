# Combat simulator: rules and data

The simulator (`tools/sim.js`, the Combat Simulator page on the site) plays GURPS 4e combat second by second on a hex map and repeats the battle many times. This page is the contract between the engine and the data: what the engine models, which Basic Set rule it follows, and what data it reads.

## Map and movement

- Hex grid, 1 yard per hex (B384), axial coordinates. The two sides deploy facing each other at the chosen distance; each unit deploys in a line with models 2 yards apart unless it has a formation setting.
- Every model has a facing (six directions). Front arc: the front hex and the two front-side hexes; side: the two flank hexes; rear: the back hex (B385). An attack from a side hex gives the defender −2 to active defences; from the rear, no active defence (B390–391).
- Movement costs 1 per hex. A model may pass through a friend's hex for +1 movement point but not stop there (B368), so a mob flows through its own front rank. A model closing to melee searches for the nearest free hex beside any foe, so a mob surrounds its targets instead of queueing. A mover faces the way it walks; after a move that used more than half its Move it may turn only one hex-side at the end (B386-387), so a model that runs past a foe can end with its side or back to it. Moving backward still costs 1 a hex (B387 charges 2; not modelled).
- **Sprinting** (B354): a model that spends consecutive seconds on straight Moves (advancing, closing in, searching) gets +20% Move (at least +1) from the second second. **Enhanced Move (Ground)** (B52) replaces that: it accelerates by its Basic Move each second up to its top speed, ×2 per level (Custodes and Genestealers ×2, Astartes, Hormagaunts and Aeldari ×1.5). Move and Attack, All-Out Attack and steps use plain Move.
- **Steps and All-Out Attack** (B368, B385): a step is Move/10 rounded up (so Move 11+ steps 2 yards); an All-Out Attack moves up to half Move rounded up, at least 2. Kneeling to standing is the step of a maneuver (B364): the model rises and acts, but can't also move that turn; lying to kneeling is a whole Change Posture.
- **Turn order** (B363) is fixed for the fight: Basic Speed, then DX, then a roll made at the start.
- Terrain on open ground: an optional cover setting per side. Light cover hides the legs, feet and groin behind it; heavy cover the torso and vitals too, and half of the arms and hands (see Cover below).
- **Facility battlefield** (user direction; "Battlefield" setting, with a layout number). A generated complex about 64 × 37 yards: a staging bay at each end where the sides deploy, three hallways three yards wide running its length, cross corridors in pieces, rooms off them through two-yard doors, and crates (light cover, DR 15) and barricades (heavy cover, DR 60) in the rooms, bays and hallways. The same layout number always gives the same facility, and every run of a Monte Carlo uses it.
  - Walls block movement and line of sight. Crates and barricades don't block sight, and nobody can stand on one, but they can be crossed (B352): an obstacle 3 or more SM smaller than the model is jumped as part of a Move for +1 movement point; anything bigger takes a whole turn and a DX roll, falling on a failure. A crate counts as SM −3 (a Marine or Ork hops it, a Gretchin climbs it), a barricade as SM −2 (hopped by SM +1 and up: Custodes, Terminators, Warbosses, Tyranid Warriors and bigger).
  - **Doors** (two yards wide, in every room's doorways) start shut or open at random. A shut door blocks movement and sight; opening or closing one is a Ready (B382), so a model walking into a shut door stops there and opens it. A model that a foe is shooting at through a doorway may shut the door instead of acting, when that's safer than anything else it could do; nobody can shut a door on a foe standing in or beside it.
  - **Breachable walls and doors** (B558): interior walls are plasteel partitions, DR 50 and 60 HP per yard; doors DR 30 and 40 HP; the outer wall can't be breached. Both are Homogeneous (B380): piercing does a fraction of its damage, so bolters barely mark a wall while krak grenades, plasma, melta and power weapons break through. A blast also batters walls and doors beside it at a third of its damage, with no armour divisor. A breached hex becomes open floor. The AI breaches when the nearest foe is out of sight, the way round is longer than the way through, and breaking the wall (a Ready and a throw for a grenade) is no slower than walking round; it values the breach by the shot or the charge it opens. On the generated layouts this is rare: foes out of sight are almost always round a corner, where walking is as quick. Line of sight is a straight hex line; if either of the two lines nudged off the hex edges is clear, the figures see each other (so a figure at a corner or in a doorway can shoot and be shot).
  - Cover is positional: a figure with a crate in the next hex toward the shooter has that crate's cover (waist-high: legs, feet and groin; a barricade is chest-high and half hides the torso, vitals, arms and hands); one whose line of sight only one of the two lines clears (a corner or a door frame) has half of its body behind the wall. Cover counts against suppression fire too.
  - No shot, thrown grenade, suppression or cone goes through a wall; a blast and its fragments stop at walls; a grenade that would land inside a wall goes off at its target.
  - Movement follows the walking distance round the walls (a shortest-path field from the goal, counting the cost of crates for each size of model). Models with nobody in sight advance toward the nearest foe on foot; models with guns may move to a spot beside a crate or a corner that has a line of fire and cover against the foes in sight, valued like any other move (the shot it gives next turn less the risk of standing there, where the risk counts only foes that can see the spot and the cover it gives). Squad behaviour (firing lines, Wait on a doorway, suppression down a hallway, spreading fire) comes from the same decision layer.
- Posture: standing or prone. Prone: −4 to melee attacks, −3 to defences, and ranged attacks against the model are at −2; standing up is a Change Posture maneuver (B551).

## Turn and maneuvers

Every model acts once per second in Basic Speed order (ties random), choosing one maneuver (B363–366):

- **Move** (full Move), **Move and Attack** (ranged: −2 or Bulk, no Acc; melee: −4, skill capped at 9, B365), **Attack**, **Aim** (+Acc, +1 more after 2 turns and +2 after 3, B364), **Feint** (Quick Contest of weapon skill; margin comes off the foe's next defence, B365), **All-Out Attack** (Determined: +4 melee / +1 ranged; Double: two attacks; Strong: +2 damage or +1/die; no active defence until next turn, B365), **All-Out Defense** (Increased: +2 to the one defence the model will use, B366), **Ready** (reload, clear a jam, pick up a weapon), **Change Posture** (a prone model rises to a kneel, then stands, B551; an Acrobatics roll springs straight up; kneeling is −2 to melee attacks and defences and −2 to be hit by ranged fire), **Concentrate** (psychic powers that need it).
- A shooter whose aimed roll would be under 8 closes the range first; low-RoF weapons re-aim when the unaimed roll is under 10; the "advance" stance fires on the move only while that shot is 8 or better.
- Shooters spread their fire: a foe that squad-mates are already shooting counts for less (expected harm ÷ (1 + shooters on it)), so a squad doesn't pour every shot into one model. A shooter aims only when the aimed roll hits more than 1.8 times as often as a shot now, since aiming costs a turn.
- **Decision layer.** Each turn a model lists every option open to it (each weapon at each of the nearest foes, fired now or after an Aim, Move and Attack, closing without firing, suppression fire, Ready and throw a grenade, Wait, reload, Concentrate; in reach: a blow, Rapid Strike, All-Out Attack Determined, Double, Strong or with Mighty Blows, Feint, a point-blank shot, two weapons at once, grab, Shove, All-Out Defense; out of reach: charge with Move and Attack, All-Out Attack after a half move, or Slam, or just close in) and scores every one on the same scale:
  value = aggression × (share of a foe knocked out × that foe's threat × horizon) − caution × (chance I'm knocked out next turn × my threat × horizon) + later payoffs discounted 0.8 per turn.
  A foe's threat is the share of an average enemy's HP it takes off per turn; the share knocked out is expected injury (hit chance, the defence it will roll, armour, wounding, Damage Reduction, averaged over hit locations or the chosen one) over the injury still needed to put it down, so overkill is worth nothing and a wounded foe is a better target. Incoming harm counts each foe's reach, range and skill against my defences in the posture the option leaves me in (none after All-Out Attack, +2 after All-Out Defense), split across the friends it could pick instead of me. Guns are valued over their reload cycle. A foe that squad-mates are already aiming at counts for less. All-Out Defense is only offered against hand-to-hand threats, and it's worth the risk it saves only as far as squad-mates could put the threatening foe down in the next couple of turns (it buys time; it doesn't win). Weights per faction live in `data/sim/ai.yaml` (aggression, caution, melee, ranged, focus, noise, Drukhari "prey", a liking for wounded victims, and "zeal" for Orks and Tyranids, who discount a distant melee less, half-ignore the fire on the way in and always think the fight is worth having); stance scales the melee and ranged terms (charge ×1.6 melee, shoot ×1.2 ranged).

## Attacks

- **Target location** (B398–400): by default only elites call shots (user direction): a model whose best combat skill (weapon or power) is 17+ with IQ 8+. Everyone else hits random locations (B552) with the framework's 1-in-6 eye-lens rule. Settings let everyone aim (RAW) or no one. An aiming attacker chooses where to hit, weighing the penalty against the damage: torso 0, vitals −3 (imp, pi, tight-beam burn ×3), skull −7 (×4, DR +2), eye −9, or −10 behind a helmet lens or visor (at the helmet's eye DR, three-quarters of its skull DR; only piercing, impaling and tight-beam burning attacks may aim there, B399), face −5, neck −5, arm or leg −2, hand −4, foot −4, groin −3. An aiming attacker may also go for a chink in the armour (B400): −8 on the torso, −10 elsewhere, halving the worn armour's DR (not natural DR).
- **Line of fire** (B389): a hex line is drawn from shooter to target; every figure on it, friend or foe, costs −4 to hit. Each round that misses (other than an explosive, which scatters) may hit the wrong target (B389): each figure on the line, then those fighting beside the target if it's in a close combat (B392), then the first figure beyond it within range, closest first, is attacked at a flat 9 or what the shooter would need to hit it on purpose, whichever is worse, and may defend as it would against a shot at it; the first that's hit or defends ends the round's flight. A round the target dodges goes on to whoever is beyond it the same way. Drilled squads don't hit each other by mistake. Kneeling and prone figures don't block the line (shot over), and neither does a friend of the shooter's in the next hex (fired past); both are house rules. A shooter holding its ground with squad-mates behind it kneels to fire at the start of each second, so the rear rank shoots over the front, and rises when a foe comes within 3 yards, when it moves or when its squad charges (kneeling to or from standing is the step of a maneuver, B364). The AI counts the penalty and the chance of hitting a friend.
- **Automatic fire** (the user's "Progressive Recoil" house rule, replacing B373): every round of a burst is rolled on its own and can hit, crit or miss separately; the 4e rapid-fire bonus and "one extra hit per Recoil of margin" aren't used. Round n after the first is at −n × Rcl (a bolter's third round is at −6). Only true laser weapons with Rcl 1, marked "1L" in the data (lascannon, multi-laser, lasblaster, scatter laser), take a flat −1 on every round after the first instead (user direction); everything else stacks, so a lasgun's tenth round is at −9 and a shuriken catapult's at −9 too. Aim (Acc and the extra seconds) counts on the first round only. Braced (a mount or bipod line; firing from prone; a crate, barricade or wall in the next hex toward the target; or kneeling behind a carried shield, all without moving that turn), Rcl is halved (rounding up, still progressive even at 1) and every round gets +1; a flat-recoil laser keeps its flat −1 and gains the +1. The AI counts all of this in its estimates. The whole burst goes at one target (spreading it over neighbours, B373, would only put the later rounds at worse odds). The Dodge still removes 1 + margin of the hits that aren't criticals. Suppression fire keeps B409, rapid-fire bonus and all: it's the one deliberate exception to the Progressive Recoil rule (user decision), since volume of fire is the point of it, and it now pins as well (see Under fire).
- Ranged: range penalty (B550), with a target's speed (the yards it moved on its last turn) added to the range only above Move 10; slower targets' movement is left to their Dodge, as B373 recommends. Min ST, half damage at or beyond ½D (B378). Shots, reloads (Ready maneuvers), regrowing bio-weapon ammunition.
- **Overpenetration** (B408-409): a piercing, impaling or tight-beam burning shot whose basic damage beats its target's cover DR (DR front and back plus HP; half HP if Unliving, a quarter if Homogeneous) goes on to the first figure behind it, as a wrong-target attack, less that cover DR.
- **Posture** (B548, B551): a kneeling or lying target is −2 to hit in the torso, vitals, groin, legs and feet, and −2 to random-location fire; an aimed head or arm shot takes no posture penalty. Random hits on a lying figure can't land on the groin, legs or feet; they strike the torso.
- **Malfunction** (B407-408): a roll at or above the weapon's Malf rolls the Malfunction table. 3-4 and 15-18 are a mechanical or electrical problem: the gun is out for the fight. 9-11 is a stoppage: the first round fires, then the gun jams, and each attempt to clear it is three Readies and a roll against Armoury or the IQ-based weapon skill −4; a beam weapon's stoppage is a mechanical problem instead. Anything else is a misfire: no shot, a Ready to find the fault, then three Readies and Armoury +2 per attempt (Armoury defaults to IQ−5). A model in melee fights on with its blade and leaves the gun. A critical failure with a weapon marked `overheat` damages the firer as the data says (plasma).
- **Weapon ST** (B270): a melee weapon's minimum ST costs −1 skill per point short, and its damage uses no more than three times that ST. A bipod weapon (`B`) needs its listed ST standing and two-thirds of it fired prone, where it counts as braced; a mounted weapon (`M`) is always fired from its mount, so ST doesn't matter.
- **Encumbrance** (B17): each loadout's armour, weapons, grenades and carried shield are weighed against Basic Lift (Lifting ST squared ÷ 5): Light (up to 2× BL) is Move ×0.8 and Dodge −1, Medium ×0.6 and −2, Heavy ×0.4 and −3, Extra-Heavy ×0.2 and −4. Power armour, battlesuits and mounted guns carry themselves. With the current loadouts that puts Guardsmen, Tempestus Scions, Fire Warriors, Skitarii, the Sister of Silence, the Incubus and the Combat Servitor at Light (a Guardsman goes from Move 6 to 4, Dodge 10 to 9).
- **Agony** (B428): an Agoniser strike that penetrates calls for HT at the weapon's penalty (+3 with High Pain Threshold); failure is Severe Pain for the rest of the fight (−4 to skills, −2 with High Pain Threshold), and a critical failure stuns as well. Machines are immune. Other affliction lines (flash, EMP, entanglement) aren't modelled; no loadout carries them.
- **Cover** (B407, Tactical Shooting p. 28-29): a shooter firing at random locations takes no penalty; a hit on a location the cover hides strikes the cover and faces its DR first, and a half-hidden location (a barricade's torso, a corner's everything) strikes it on 4-6 on 1d. An aimed shot at an exposed location takes only its location penalty; at a hidden or half-hidden one, −2 more and the cover's DR if the location is hidden. A figure firing a two-handed gun from cover exposes its arms and hands and half its torso. Crates hide the legs, feet and groin behind DR 15; barricades hide those behind DR 60 and half the torso and arms; a corner half of everything but the head; the open-ground heavy setting everything below the neck but half the arms. What cover costs to shoot out from it is unchanged (TS p. 28): nothing behind light cover, −2 behind a barricade, −4 behind heavy cover, nothing for a shooter who is braced and aiming. Crates and barricades are semi-ablative (B559): every 10 points of damage they stop wears 1 DR off, and at 0 they're gone (the replay shows the rubble).
- **Drilled squads** (TS p. 22-23, 37): factions marked `drilled` in `data/sim/ai.yaml` (Astartes, Custodes, Sororitas, Mechanicus, Imperial Guard, Aeldari, T'au, Necrons) fire past a drilled friend at −2 instead of −4 and never hit one by mistake; the AI knows it.
- **Follow-up shots** (TS p. 14): after an aimed shot, later shots at the same target keep half the weapon's Acc (all of it braced at RoF 1) on their first round, until the shooter moves, defends, or changes target or weapon. Any active defence spoils Aim and follow-up aim (B364), so an aiming model lets a shot come rather than dodge when its Dodge is under a 30% chance or the shot can barely hurt it.
- **Close-contact shots** (TS p. 25): All-Out Attack (Determined) with a gun at a foe in reach is +4, not +1; a gun held close in is −2 to parry (a handgun) or −1 (a long arm). An aimed pistol shot held in both hands counts as braced (TS p. 12) when the off hand is free.
- **Sighted shots** (TS p. 13-14, an option, off by default: `sightedShots`): a shot that uses Aim is All-Out Attack (Determined), +1 and no defence until the model's next turn.
- **Under fire** (Tactical Shooting p. 21, 34): at the start of its turn a model that since its last turn was caught in suppression fire, missed by 2 or less, inside a blast (2 yards per die), wounded, or saw a friend within 5 yards go down makes one Fright Check (with the heat-of-battle +5 but no cap of 13, by user direction: superhuman nerve is rewarded, so only a 17 or 18 breaks an Astartes; the casualty morale checks are uncapped too, and only Shadow in the Warp keeps the cap), at minus the volume of fire it took (the rapid-fire table used as a measure of it) unless it's in cover; failure rolls the Fright Check Table. A model shot at while in cover then rolls Will−2 unless it has Combat Reflexes, and on a failure keeps its head down: it may only reload, ready, defend, work a door or stay down that turn. Unfazeable models, Necrons and mindless models ignore both.
- **Fire and movement** (TS p. 21): a shooting squad on the advance (not zealots or chargers) pairs off each second; one of each pair covers (leaning to fire, Aim and suppression) while the other bounds (leaning to moving up and into cover), swapping every two seconds. A foe that a covering friend is shooting at, or that sits in a friend's suppression zone, counts as shooting back at less than full effect, by the chance it keeps its head down or freezes.
- **Suppression as area denial** (TS p. 18): a gunner may suppress a lone foe in cover or in a doorway, not only a cluster of three, and values the zone for the fire it stops as well as the harm it does.
- **Corners and doorways** (TS p. 23-24): a model with no foe in sight but one that means to come on within about two moves takes a Wait covering the approach and shoots the first foe to step into view and range; after three quiet seconds it goes to look. A model choosing cover avoids hugging a corner a hidden foe could come round and reach it at. Grenades can be lobbed through a doorway or round a corner at a hex the thrower can see and the foe can see (Attacking an area, B414: +4, no defence, anyone there may dive for cover; a miss lands its margin of failure in yards off, at most half the distance).
- **Peeling** (TS p. 22-23): cautious line troops (Guard, T'au, Aeldari and the like) that see more than about three times their own threat fall back by pairs out of sight and hold there, going back on the advance after 20 seconds.
- **Reloading** (TS p. 20): a model with nobody in sight tops up a magazine that's under 60% full; one that runs dry under fire steps out of sight first if it can within a move.
- **What each side knows** (TS p. 11, 21, 27-28; facility maps, the page's "Knowledge" setting): a side knows only the foes some friend has seen in the last 5 seconds, and a model that attacks gives itself away (B548). With nobody known, a model heads for where a foe was last seen, then the enemy's staging bay, then sweeps the facility; a unit holding in the shooting stance waits 10 quiet seconds first. "Everyone sees everything" restores the old perfect knowledge, and open ground always has it (nothing blocks sight there).
- **Ambush** (TS p. 21, B393; a per-unit checkbox): the unit can't be seen until it attacks or a foe comes within 5 yards. When it springs, every foe who hadn't seen it rolls IQ (+6 with Combat Reflexes) or loses its next turn. An ambush that nobody walks into gives up after a minute and goes looking.
- **Slicing the pie** (TS p. 23-24): in a facility with limited knowledge, a mover that isn't a zealot or charging stops at the step that first shows it a foe within 10 yards, so the two meet at the corner with it as light cover for both. If the foe wasn't holding a Wait, a Quick Contest of Per (+1 for Combat Reflexes) decides who acts first; a foe that wins shoots at once and loses its next turn.
- **Stealth** (B222, B47, B393; user direction), with limited knowledge (facilities by default). A model that has something to hide behind from a viewer (cover from it, lying down, a chameleon hide, or 20 yards or more) and hasn't attacked this second or the last isn't seen automatically: each second the viewing side makes one Quick Contest, its best Perception or Observation (plus Acute Vision) with the range penalty and the target's SM, against the target's Stealth (DX−5 untrained), −5 if it moved more than half its Move. A Lictor's Chameleonic Hide takes its level off the roll when it keeps still and half that when it moves, as its notes say; the Mandrake's Chameleon adds +2 a level in dim or dark hexes. Darkness (−3 or worse) is itself concealment. A model anyone can see in the open is seen at once, and a model already seen stays seen while it's in someone's sight. A foe that slipped out of sight and moved is only a last known position, which searchers head for; one that stayed put is remembered for 5 seconds. Gunfire gives the shooter away to every foe within 40 yards, walls or not; blades, throws, powers and quiet guns (splinter, shuriken, needle, bows, Kroot rifles) only to foes who can see it. An attack by a model the foes hadn't seen is a surprise attack: foes who see it or are within 10 yards roll IQ (+6 with Combat Reflexes) or lose their next turn.
- **Lighting** (B394; "Lighting" setting, facilities only): mixed by default, with the bays and hallways lit, the cross corridors dim (−2), and each room lit, dim (−3) or dark (−7), fixed by the layout number; or all lit, or dark (−7) everywhere. Darkness at a figure's hex is a penalty to spotting it, shooting or striking at it, and defending against its attacks. Night Vision takes its level off the penalty, Dark Vision ignores it, and Infravision ignores it against anything that isn't a machine (B47, B60, B71). The replay shades dim and dark hexes.
- **Stalkers** (user direction): a stealthy model (Stealth 14+ or a chameleon hide) that fights hand to hand and hasn't been seen closes at half Move once within 20 yards of its prey, so it keeps its full Stealth, takes the steps the foe can't see first and the dark ones next, and goes for a hex beside or behind its target rather than in front of it.
- **Horror** (user direction): every comrade who sees a model cut down (killed, or wounded to 0 HP or below and out of the fight) within 20 yards makes a Fright Check at −1, −1 more for every one it has already seen this fight, −2 if it happened up close in melee, −2 if the body was taken past −2×HP, and −3 if the killer struck out of hiding. Unfazeable models and those that never break ignore it.
- **Mental disadvantages** (B120-154, review batch H), each with its self-control number from the data:
  - Berserk (B124): more than HP/4 of injury in one second calls for a self-control roll (less shock); Battle Rage goes berserk on entering combat unless it makes the roll (the Skorpekh Destroyer's Berserk is priced at −15 at 12, which is Battle Rage). A berserker All-Out Attacks anything in reach, otherwise closes on the nearest foe by charge, Move and Attack or slam, otherwise fires its gun without aiming; it ignores shock and stun, keeps its Move below 1/3 HP, rolls at +4 to stay conscious and alive, and makes no Fright Checks. Each foe it downs gives a roll to snap out, after which an HT roll tells whether it stays conscious.
  - Cowardice (B129): before closing with the enemy (a charge, an advance, a grab), a self-control roll, −5 when badly hurt; failing, it takes the best option that doesn't close. In melee already, it fights. Its Fright Checks take −2 at 12 (−4 at 6, −3 at 9, −1 at 15).
  - Overconfidence (B148): choosing All-Out Defense, cover, a Wait, peeling back or spreading out needs a self-control roll; failing, it takes the best bold option.
  - Impulsiveness (B139): a Wait, holding in cover or a second turn of Aim needs a self-control roll; failing, it acts at once.
  - Bloodlust (B125): it goes for killing blows, so wounded foes count half again as much in its choice of target. The sim takes downed models off the board, so the extra shot into a downed foe has nothing to hit.
  - Slave Mentality no longer exempts drones and servitors from morale (B154 says nothing about Fright Checks); Machine Body and Machine Mind meta-traits now flag the model as a Machine (no FP), the Gun Drone included.
- **Shooting through walls and doors** (TS p. 28, B408): a foe seen within the last second on the far side of a single wall or shut door can be shot through it, at random hit location and with no defence, the structure's DR (after the armour divisor) coming off each hit and the structure taking the damage. The AI does it only when enough gets through to matter (heavy weapons, mostly).
- **Tactical Dodging** (TS p. 17, an option, off by default): each model watches the one gunman that worries it most and can dodge only that one's fire.
- **Deceptive Shot** (house rule): a shooter may take −2 to hit for each −1 to the target's Dodge (or its Parry against a point-blank shot), never below skill 10, as Deceptive Attack does in melee (B369). Not for explosives, cones, blasts or Malediction. The AI weighs it with the hit location; it rarely pays, since −2 skill also costs rapid-fire hits and a Dodge only strips 1 + margin of them.
- Melee: a weapon with more than one way of hitting (a sword's swing and thrust, with the same field setting) uses whichever does more to that foe, blow by blow; reach, Deceptive Attack optimised against the defender's best defence, Rapid Strike, Extra Attack, unbalanced weapons (no parry after attacking), point-blank fire with a gun when it beats the blade, close-combat reloads when quick.
- **Area effects** (B400, B413–414): explosive (`ex`) damage also hits every model within 2 yards per die of damage (6d×2 reaches 24 yards) at damage ÷ (3 × yards), with no armour divisor (B414), and a power bought with a higher Explosion level (B107) counts distance as yards ÷ level. Every round of an explosive burst that hits bursts on its own. Fragmentation (`[Nd]`) attacks every model within 5 × dice yards at skill 15, modified by range from the blast, SM and posture (kneeling or lying −2), and −4 for each standing figure between the blast and it, since bodies screen those behind them as in any line of fire (user direction, from B389; kneeling and lying figures don't screen); one fragment hits, plus one per 3 points of success, and the model struck directly takes at least one. Fragments hit random locations, and a location behind cover from the blast point hits the cover. Cones (`Cone N yards` in the usage) are one attack roll at the aim point; everyone in the wedge (a yard wide at the muzzle, widening to the full width at maximum range) whom the shooter can see is caught and may dive clear with a Dodge; a miss catches nobody. Cones, blasts and all explosion collateral are large-area injury (B400): the torso, at the average of the torso's DR and the least protected location's, rounded up. A missed explosive shot lands its margin of failure in yards off (at most half the distance), a dodged one the dodge's margin of success.
- **Catching fire** (B433-434): a flame (cone) or burning explosion with 3+ basic damage sets a target's clothing alight, 10+ all of it. Only clothing burns: flak, robes, wychsuits, hides and the unarmoured who wear clothes (Orks, Gretchin, T'au castes, Navigators); sealed plate, carapace, chitin and machines don't. Burning clothing does 1d−4 (part) or 1d−1 (all) a second as large-area injury and costs −2 or −3 DX; a weapon's own "per turn while ablaze" line (promethium clinging) replaces that damage and is no longer a second hit. A fire that can't get through the target's gear is ignored. Putting it out is a Ready and a DX roll; with all its clothes alight the model rolls on the ground, three Readies to an attempt. The AI weighs that against the burning it saves. Tight beams (lasers) don't ignite anything.

## Weapons in hand and close combat

- A model holds its gun, its blade, or both (B382). Both are ready when both are one-handed (a † weapon counts as one-handed with 2× its ST, ‡ with 3×, B270; between 1.5× and 2× a † weapon could be used in one hand but must be readied after each use, which the sim treats as two-handed; a gun also needs Bulk −2 or better), when they're one weapon (a bayonet on a lasgun, a guardian spear's bolt caster) or when either is a natural weapon. Otherwise a model starts with its gun (its blade on the charge stance) and switching is a Ready turn, free on a Fast-Draw roll (B194; +1 with Combat Reflexes, B43). Only a blade in hand can parry.
- A shot from within reach can be parried: the defender knocks the gun aside (all the shots miss) or dodges and blocks as in melee (B391; the exact penalty for parrying a gun hasn't been checked against the book, so none is applied).
- The Attack maneuver includes a step (B364–365): a foe one hex out of reach is attacked at full skill after the step, not with Move and Attack.

## Grappling

A charging model whose weapon can barely hurt its foe (under 1 HP of expected injury per swing) grabs it (B370): an attack with DX or Wrestling, Judo or Sumo Wrestling, defended normally. Holders then try a **takedown** (Quick Contest of the higher of ST, DX or grappling skill; the held model is at −4 DX; a grappler that loses the contest outright suffers the takedown itself, going down and losing its hold) and, with the foe on the ground, a **pin** (Quick Contest of ST). Grappling uses Lifting ST, so servo-driven armour wrestles at its full strength (B65). Several grapplers pull together with the strongest one's ST plus a fifth of each other's (a simulator rule; Basic gives no formula). A held model can't retreat, can't fire a gun, can't Feint and isn't knocked back. On the ground or pinned it spends its turn trying to break free: a Quick Contest of ST against the hold, which is +5 when the lead grappler has both hands on it, or +10 (+5 one-handed) when it's a pin, −4 if the grappler is stunned; a pinned model may try only once every 10 seconds (B371). A pinned model is helpless: no active defence, and any attacker may aim at it, chinks included. Two grapplers keep a pin; the others use All-Out Attack (Strong or Determined, B365) and Mighty Blows (1 FP for +2 damage or +1 per die, B357).

## Wait, suppression, grenades, slams and two weapons

- **Wait** (B366): a shooter that a dangerous charger (expected injury a quarter of its HP or more) will reach this turn holds its fire and shoots the charger as it steps within reach + 1 yard; a charger that is killed, stunned or knocked down there makes no attack. Only the model the charger is heading for waits. Nobody waits for a foe that is itself waiting, or for one that would rather shoot than close (two Waits would stand facing each other forever).
- **Suppression fire** (B409): a weapon of RoF 5+ may spray a 3-yard zone (a hex and its six neighbours) round a cluster of three or more foes instead of aiming, when that promises more harm. Everyone in the zone when it's laid down, friend or foe, and every foe who moves into it before the gunner's next turn, is attacked once at the gunner's effective skill with every modifier (rapid-fire bonus, SM, posture), capped at 6 + the rapid-fire bonus (B409); one hit plus one per full Rcl of margin, up to the shots fired; random locations, so cover takes the hits that land behind it; Dodge allowed. The rapid-fire bonus goes on past 99 shots at +1 per doubling. The AI counts the foes' Dodge and the friends caught in the zone, so it doesn't spray a melee.
- **Grenades** (B410): lore-standard grenades per loadout (`grenades` in `data/sim/loadouts.yaml`). Throwing range from ST and weight (B355), with +1 ST for Throwing at DX+1 and +2 at DX+2 (B356); a Ready to draw it and a second to arm it (Fast-Draw (Grenade) makes the draw free and arms it in the same Ready), then an Attack with Throwing at the range penalty. Thrown at a foe, the grenade can be dodged or blocked (B373-377); it then goes off the defence's margin of success in yards away. A grenade that isn't defended is a direct hit: full damage with its armour divisor. A foe in sight is always thrown at directly (user direction); the +4 throw at a hex with no defence (Attacking an area, B414) is kept for foes out of sight, through a doorway or round a corner, and whoever is on the hex it lands on may only dive for cover (success: a third of the blast; failure: all of it, as large-area injury with no divisor). A miss lands its margin of failure in yards off, at most half the distance, and whoever is on that hex may dive the same way. Everyone else takes the blast (B414) with no armour divisor, plus fragments. Anyone who sees a grenade land within 3 yards, or within half its fragment radius, may dive for cover (B377): a Dodge that, on a success, puts them a yard further away and prone, into a hex the blast can't see (behind a crate, a wall or a corner) when there is one; out of its sight they take neither blast nor fragments. The AI throws at whichever foe or foe's feet gives the most expected harm, counting the blast and fragments on its own side and itself.
- **Grenade sense**: the risk a model weighs for standing on a hex includes the worst grenade a foe in throwing range could land a yard or two away (blast and expected fragments, less what its cover from the thrower, kneeling or lying stops), and the more squad-mates within 2 yards of the spot, the likelier that throw. A shooter with squad-mates close and a grenade-armed foe in range may step a yard or two to spread out, valued like any move. Any mover under grenade threat (a known foe with a grenade left within a move of throwing range), charging mobs included (user direction), fans out: of the steps that gain as much ground, it takes the one with fewest squad-mates beside it, and a charge still more than a move from its foe heads for the same spot that way.
- **Slam** (B371): a charger whose weapon can't hurt its foe crashes into it instead (DX, or a grappling or unarmed skill if better; a slam from a Move and Attack takes neither its −4 nor its cap of 9; All-Out Attack still gives +4). Each side takes HP × yards moved ÷ 100 dice of crushing damage (a fraction of half a die or more rounds up; below one die, 1d−3, 1d−2 or 1d−1). Whoever rolls twice the other's damage knocks the other down; otherwise, if the slammer did at least as much, the target rolls DX or falls. Slams knock down, never back. A mob uses this to put a Marine on the ground for the pin.
- **Hurting yourself** (B379): a punch or kick at DR 3 or more does its striker 1 crushing per 5 points of basic damage (up to that DR) against its own hand or foot armour, so gauntleted Marines don't notice and Gretchin do.
- **Shove** (B372): a model that can't hurt its foe, beside a friend who can, shoves it: knockback from double its thrust damage, DX to keep its feet.
- **Two weapons at once** (B417): a one-handed melee weapon and a pistol (Bulk −2 or better) attack in the same turn, each at −4 (reduced by the Dual-Weapon Attack technique), the off hand at −4 more without Ambidexterity or Off-Hand Weapon Training. Used when it beats a single attack.
- **Feverish Defense** (extra effort, B357): 1 FP for +2 to a Dodge, Parry or Block against an attack whose expected injury is a fifth of the defender's HP or more, while the defender has more than a third of its FP (minimum 3) left.

## Defence

Dodge, Parry and Block (B374–377; Block from Shield skill / 2 + 3, Combat Reflexes and Enhanced Block). Side attacks −2, rear attacks get no defence; stunned −4 and no retreat, and a model that shakes off stun keeps the −4 until its next turn (B364); kneeling −2, prone −3, off balance −2.

- **Limits per turn** run from one of the defender's turns to its next (B363): one retreat, one Block, and −4 for each parry after the first (−2 with a fencing weapon or Weapon Master or Trained by a Master, −1 with both; B376).
- **Retreat** (B377): against melee attacks only, +3 Dodge and +1 Parry or Block (+3 Parry with a fencing weapon, or bare-handed with Boxing, Judo or Karate). The defender steps a yard back or aside into a free hex, and keeps the bonus against every later attack by the same foe until its next turn. No retreat with nowhere to go, while held, stunned or kneeling, or after its own Move and Attack or Slam; a prone model can roll back.
- **Move and Attack** (B366), melee or firing on the move: no parry and no retreat until the model's next turn.
- **Parrying**: bare hands are −3 against a weapon unless it's a thrust or the defender knows Judo or Karate (B377). A weapon that parries a punch, a claw or a grab strikes the attacker's limb if the parrier then makes a skill roll (−4 against Judo or Karate) (B376). Nobody parries a Slam or Shove from a body heavier than their Basic Lift (twice that with a two-handed weapon; the attacker counts as its ST in pounds, B376). A gun fired at point-blank range can be dodged or parried, but not blocked, and gives no retreat (B375-377).
- **Thrown grenades** can be dodged or blocked (B373-375); the grenade then lands the defence's margin of success in yards away.
- **Dodge and Drop** against ranged attacks (+3, goes prone, B377).
- **Shields** (B287, B484, Damage to Shields in use, so this is the book's rule): a carried shield's Defense Bonus counts against every attack, firearms and beams included, but only from the front and the shield side (the left). A shield fills the off hand, so its bearer can't hold a gun and a blade at once unless they're one weapon, and can't use two-handed weapons without letting go. When a defence succeeds only because of the DB, the attack struck the shield: a storm shield's field takes it first, then the shield's DR (after the armour divisor); damage through DR costs the shield HP as a Homogeneous object, and damage over DR + HP/4 punches through to the bearer (the shield arm on 1-2 on 1d, otherwise where the attack was aimed). Against a burst, a Dodge that needed the DB puts every round it turned aside into the shield; otherwise DB of them. At 0 HP the shield rolls HT 12 each time it's used or disables (no DB, no Block); at each further −1×HP it rolls HT or is smashed, and at −5×HP it's smashed outright, at −10×HP torn away. A crushing or cutting blow the shield stops still knocks its bearer back (B484). The storm shield's and dispersion shield's fields cover only the front and shield side too. The AI goes round a shield: a melee attacker picks a hex on the foe's weapon side or behind it when that's no more than two steps further. Shield DR and HP (Combat Shield 50/40, Boarding 60/60, Storm 100/65, Praesidium 150/60, Dispersion 100/40) are simulator values scaled to this project's armour, to be confirmed. Carried shields are chosen per unit on the simulator page (no default loadout carries one).

**Critical hits and misses** (B556-557). A critical hit rolls 3d on the Critical Hit Table: 3 or 18 triple damage, 4 or 17 DR halved, 5 or 16 double damage, 6 or 15 maximum damage, 7, 13 or 14 a major wound if anything penetrates, 8 double shock (a limb or extremity hit is a "funny-bone" hit: the hand lets go or the leg folds, no lasting cripple), 12 the victim drops its weapon. Critical hits to the skull, face or eye use the Critical Head Blow Table instead: 3 maximum damage ignoring DR, 4-5 DR halved and a major wound, 6-7 the blow finds an eye (from the front or side; otherwise as 4), 8 the victim must Do Nothing next turn, 14 drops its weapon, 15 maximum, 16 double, 17 DR halved, 18 triple.

A critical miss with a blade or a gun rolls the Critical Miss Table: 3, 4, 17 and 18 break the weapon, but firearms (other than beam weapons such as lasguns, plasma, melta and gauss), crushing weapons and fine weapons (power, force and relic weapons) roll again and break only on a second "broken" result, otherwise dropping it; 5 hits the attacker for full damage and 6 for half, but piercing, impaling and ranged attacks reroll and only hit on a second 5 or 6; 7 and 13 leave it off balance (−2 to defences); 8 and 12 turn the weapon in the hand (a Ready); 9-11 and 14 drop it; 15 strains the shoulder, so that weapon is out of the fight; 16 falls down (a shooter loses its balance instead). Claws, fists, slams, grabs and melee powers use the Unarmed Critical Miss Table: 3 and 18 knock the attacker out, 4 and 17 strain a limb (1 HP and a crippled arm), 5 and 6 hit something solid for its own thrust crushing (half on a 6), 7 and 14 stumble past the foe and end facing away, 8 and 16 fall, 9-11 lose balance, 12 a DX roll or fall, 13 drop its guard, 15 tear a muscle (1d−3 injury, off balance). A ranged attack is a critical miss only on 18, or 17 at effective skill 15 or less (B382); a gun with a Malf number malfunctions on its own table instead.

Defence rolls crit too (B381-382): a critical success against a melee attack sends the attacker to the Critical Miss Table; a botched Dodge falls down, a botched Block loses the shield (and its Defense Bonus) until a Ready, and a botched parry rolls the Critical Miss Table for the defender's weapon.

Success rolls follow B343-348: 3 and 4 always succeed, 17 and 18 always fail, and no attack is rolled at effective skill below 3 (a burst stops at the round that would need it). Quick Contests go to whoever succeeds, or to the smaller failure when both fail (a tie changes nothing). Resisted Maledictions use the Rule of 16.

## Size (Pyramid 3/77, "Combat Writ Large")

- **Melee to-hit** (p. 7): the smaller fighter adds the SM difference to its melee attacks (at most +4); the larger subtracts it. A Carnifex (SM +2) is −2 to hit a Marine, who is +2 to hit it.
- **Grappling** (B370, p. 9-10): +1 per SM of difference to grab, either way, at most +4. A smaller grappler can only take down or pin a larger foe if it grabs a part where that bonus plus the grappling penalty comes to 0 or less, so it goes for the neck (−3); against a foe more than 3 SM bigger it can only hang on. Anyone the foe out-muscles more than twice over is only extra encumbrance (B370) and is shrugged off. In the pin contest the larger fighter gets +3 per SM of difference.
- **Parrying big attacks** (p. 8): a claw, fist or bite counts as weighing ST x ST / 100 lb, a slam or shove ST x ST / 10 lb (Quadratic Natural Attacks), and an armed blow as the heavier of its weapon and the wielder's fist (Weapon Power), against the parrier's Basic Lift and for breaking the parrying weapon.
- **Huge slams** (p. 9): against a slam or shove by something 3 or more SM bigger, the only defence is diving aside, and shields don't help.
- **Not modelled**: multi-hex figures, reach from size, height effects on hit locations, trampling, evading and climbing big foes, and the special attacks table.

## Martial Arts options

The melee layer follows GURPS Martial Arts (MA) where it adds to or clarifies the Basic Set:

- **Committed Attack** (MA99): +2 to hit, or +1 damage per two dice; the attacker's defences are −2 until its next turn, with no retreat and no parry. A long weapon can strike and then step back out of a shorter foe's reach ("attack and fly out").
- **Defensive Attack** (MA100): −2 damage or −1 per die, and +1 to a parry or block until the next turn; it lets an unbalanced weapon parry after swinging.
- **All-Out Attack (Feint)** (MA97): a feint, then a blow at the lowered defence. **All-Out Attack (Double) with a Rapid Strike** gives three blows, two of them at −6 (−3 for masters). A Rapid Strike may open with a feint (MA127).
- **Telegraphic Attack** (MA113): +4 to hit, +2 to every defence against it, with the critical range of the unmodified skill; open to anyone, weighed against Deceptive Attack for each blow and each hit location.
- **Feints** (B365, MA100-101): the AI values a feint's margin at about 1.2 plus 0.7 per point of skill edge. A **Beat** is a ST-based feint against the Parry or Block that turned the beater's blow (or that it parried), and it lowers that defence against every attacker until the end of the beater's next turn. A **Ruse** is IQ-based, resisted by Per. A **Defensive Feint** takes its margin off the foe's next attack instead.
- **Evaluate** (B364, MA100): +1 a turn up to +3 on the next blow at that foe, and the bonus cancels that foe's feint and Deceptive Attack penalties against the evaluator.
- **Riposte** (MA124): a model with an effective Parry of 14+ facing a single foe takes up to −4 on its parry; if the parry holds, that foe's next defence against it is lowered as much.
- **Trained fighters only** (MA113): feints, Committed and Defensive Attack, Deceptive Attack and Rapid Strike need at least DX level in the weapon's skill.
- **Extra blows move on** (MA127, B370): a blow left over when its target falls goes to another foe in reach, losing one blow for each hex skipped between the two.
- **Two weapons at one foe** (B417): it defends at −1 against both.
- **Weights and parries** (B376): a weapon's weight comes from its item (a limb, claw or fist weighs ST/10 lb); a weapon heavier than the parrier's Basic Lift (twice with a two-handed weapon) can't be parried, and a parried weapon three or more times the parrying weapon's weight breaks it on 2 in 6, one more per extra multiple, one less for fine (power and force) weapons.
- **Retreat** (MA123-124): a step straight back gets the full +3/+1; a step to the side is a Sideslip at one less.
- **Held models** (MA117, B391): −4 for being grappled and −4 more per yard of the weapon's reach (reach C weapons and fists only the −4), the Parry half that.
- **Tip Slash** (MA113): a weapon that thrusts to impale may swing its tip for cutting at its impaling damage −2.
- **Grappling** (MA114): a foe on All-Out Attack loses takedown and pin contests outright; a Committed attacker is −2 in them. Extra arms (MA115; the Mechanicus servo-arm and manipulator mechadendrite, not weak or weapon-mount arms) give +2 each to grab, to hold a victim against breaking free and to break free, and more arms than the foe gives +3 to pin it or resist its pin. A trained grappler (Wrestling, Judo or Sumo above DX) about to lose a takedown can Sprawl (MA119): it goes down willingly, the contest runs at +3 for it, and a grappler that loses or ties goes down too and loses its hold.
- **Flesh Hooks** (Lictor, Ravener; bio-weapons.yaml): a reach-2 hook strike (Brawling, thr imp). A hit gives an immediate grapple at no extra attack and no defence (the hooks already hold) that drags the victim beside the bearer; the Lictor's Extra Attack then goes into it with the claws.
- **Stop thrust** (B366): a thrust from a melee Wait adds +1 damage per two full yards the charger ran.
- **Acrobatic Stand** (MA98): Acrobatics at −6 (and encumbrance) to spring up.
- **Mighty Blows and Feverish Defense** (MA131): Mighty Blows goes with a plain Attack only, not All-Out Attack, and Feverish Defense isn't open to a model on Committed or All-Out Attack.
- **Options, off by default**: −1 per extra dodge in a turn (MA122; masters exempt) and cinematic extra effort (MA132; Heroic Charge: 1 FP to ignore Move and Attack's −4 and skill cap of 9).
- **Not modelled**: Counterattack and Targeted Attack (no template carries the techniques), Stop Hits, Slip and Dive, Defensive and Reversed Grip, Harsh Realism for unarmed fighters, the other cinematic skills, and MA's realistic-injury options.

## Injury

Standard GURPS HP (B377–420) or the user's Revised Fractional Health (see the rules document on the user's Drive; five boxes per level, multi-box hits). Both use armour divisors, Weak Points, regenerating shields, wounding multipliers, Injury Tolerance, Damage Reduction, follow-ups, and:

- **Shields**: a regenerating shield that's up absorbs each hit before armour; whatever gets through keeps its follow-up. Attackers don't aim at locations or chinks while it's up.
- **Follow-ups** (B381): a follow-up (a bolt's explosive core, an inferno bolt's burn, a toxin) applies only when the main hit penetrates, and ignores DR. An explosive follow-up bursts inside the body: triple damage (B414, internal explosion), then its wounding multiplier.
- **Crippling** (B420–421): a limb or extremity takes no more injury in all than it needs to be crippled, so repeated shots at a crippled hand stop hurting (and the AI stops aiming there); a crippled arm drops what it holds (a two-handed weapon becomes unusable); a crippled leg or foot drops the model prone and it can only crawl.
- **Knockback** (B378): crushing damage, and cutting damage that fails to penetrate, moves the target 1 yard per full (ST−2) of basic damage and it must roll DX or fall prone.
- **Below 1/3 HP** (B419, standard HP): Move and Dodge are halved, rounding up.
- **Shock** (B419): −1 per HP of injury, or per full HP/10 for models with 20+ HP, at most −4.
- **Major wounds** (B420): a single injury over HP/2, or any crippling, calls for an HT roll; failure stuns and knocks down, failure by 5+ knocks out.
- **Blunt trauma** (B379, extended by user direction): armour that stops a hit still passes some of its force as crushing injury. Flexible armour passes 1 HP per full 5 points of crushing damage, or per full 10 of impaling or piercing (B379). A cutting blow that doesn't get through lands as a crushing one, so it counts at the crushing rate (user direction). Rigid armour passes crushing force too, at 1 HP per full 10 points (a simulator value, half the flexible rate; the Basic Set gives rigid armour none). Burning, toxic and explosive damage pass nothing this way. The AI's damage estimates include it.
- **Weapon Master and Trained by a Master** (B93, B99): Rapid Strike at −3 and extra parries at −2; Weapon Master adds +1 per die of the ST-based damage at skill DX+1, +2 at DX+2.
- **Deceptive Attack** (B369) may not take effective skill below 10.
- **Extra Attack** (B53) adds to All-Out Attack (Double) and Rapid Strike.
- **Bleeding** (B420): every minute, a model below full HP rolls HT or loses 1 HP (standard mode).
- Shock, knockdown and stun, consciousness and death checks, Hard to Kill, Reanimation Protocols.
- **Morale** is a Fright Check (B360) for every model still standing when its unit falls to half and to a quarter strength: Will + Fearlessness + 5 (the heat of battle) + 2 for Combat Reflexes, with no cap (user direction; B360 caps it at 13), so only 17-18 always fail. A failure rolls 3d plus the margin of failure on the Fright Check Table (B360-361): low results stun for a second or until a Will roll, middling ones stun for 1d or 2d seconds, cost FP or leave it retching, 21 panics it into flight, and 17+ otherwise takes it out of the fight. The unit routs only when no model in it is left fighting. Unfazeable, Necrons and mindless models don't check. Mental stun is shaken off on Will (or IQ, +6 with Combat Reflexes, after Perils of the Warp); physical stun on HT.
- **Knockdown and stun** (B420): an HT roll on a major wound and on any head (skull, face, eye) or vitals hit that causes shock; the −10 (skull, eye) and −5 (face, vitals) apply only to major wounds and not to models without a brain or vitals; a knocked-down model drops what it holds. **Death checks** (B419): failing by 1-2 is a mortal wound (out of the fight; Necrons reanimate from it), and a success that needed Hard to Kill leaves the model collapsed, apparently dead (B58). **Shock** totals the turn's injury before dropping fractions for 20+ HP, and a "double shock" critical raises the cap to −8.
- **Hit locations** (B398-400, B552): chinks in armour, eyes and vitals can only be targeted by piercing, impaling or tight-beam burning attacks; a random eye hit by anything else counts as a skull hit without the skull's extra DR. An aimed attack at the eye, skull, face, neck, groin or vitals that misses by exactly 1 hits the torso. Natural DR protects the eye as fully as the head unless a trait gives an eye figure (user direction, so a hide isn't beaten by eye shots alone; the book gives no figure). No Brain treats the skull as the face (no extra DR); only No Vitals makes the vitals a torso hit. Armour divisors drop DR to 0 at the bottom, not 1 (B378).
- **Crippled limbs** (B421): a crippled arm or hand drops what it holds; the weapon goes to the other hand at −4 (none with Ambidexterity), two-handed weapons can't be used, and a crippled shield arm loses the shield. No blanket −4 on every attack. A crippled leg drops the model (no halved Dodge in standard HP).
- **Knockback** (B378): from any crushing hit, ranged included (not explosions), resisted with the best of DX, Acrobatics or Judo, +4 with Perfect Balance. An explosive follow-up whose carrier doesn't penetrate still bursts against that armour (B381). Only damage that gets past cover can bruise through armour (B379). Diffuse caps injury at 1 (piercing, impaling) or 2.
- **Bleeding** (B420, standard HP): once a minute, HT at −1 per 5 HP lost; a failure costs 1 HP, a critical failure 3; a critical success or three successes in a row stop it; only cutting, impaling and piercing wounds bleed, and No Blood, Diffuse and Machines don't.
- **Fatigue** (B426): below a third of its FP a model's Move and Dodge are halved; FP spent below 0 costs 1 HP each, and at −FP it collapses; at 0 FP or less it rolls Will before acting and collapses on a failure. **Extra effort** (B357): Mighty Blows (+2 or +1 a die, ST-based weapons only, with a plain Attack) and Flurry of Blows (half the Rapid Strike penalty) cost 1 FP an attack, hit or miss; Feverish Defense 1 FP; a critical failure while using any of them costs 1 HP to the arm (or leg, for a dodge), DR no help. All-Out Attack (Strong) likewise needs ST-based thrust or swing damage (B365).
- **Aim** (B364, B372): Acc plus aiming bonuses never exceed twice the weapon's Acc; any active defence spoils the aim, and an injured aimer rolls Will or loses it. A feint helps only the next attack (both blows of an All-Out Attack (Double)) and lapses if not used next turn (B365).
- **Machines** (B16, B263): Necrons have no FP to spend, so no Mighty Blows, Feverish Defense or exhaustion roll.
- **Regeneration** (B80): HP per second by tier (Extreme 10, Very Fast 1, Fast 1/60, Regular 1/3600), read from any Regeneration trait; under Fractional Health the healing clears the least severe wound box once it covers that level's threshold.

## Psychic powers

Psykers use the attack and defence powers listed for their template in `data/sim/powers.yaml` (built from the psyker traits under the project's Sorcery model). A power with Malediction (B106) is a Quick Contest of skill against the target's Will (or HT) instead of an attack and a defence, at −1 per yard (level 1), normal range penalties (level 2) or none within 200 yd (level 3). Warp powers fail against a blank (Pariah Gene). **Shadow in the Warp** (Tyranid traits): a psyker within the trait's radius of an enemy bioform (8 yards for a Ravener or Lictor, 16 for a Warrior or Zoanthrope, 32 for a Hive Tyrant) is at −3 to its powers and to the Will roll against Perils, and makes a Fright Check the first time it comes in range; failure rolls the Fright Check Table. A power is used like a weapon when its expected harm beats the psyker's weapon: it rolls its skill, pays its FP cost, and on the listed Perils trigger rolls the listed consequence. FP loss is tracked; a model at 0 FP or below rolls HT to act (B426).

## Data the engine reads

- Templates (`Library/**/*.gct` via the site data): attributes, skills, traits and combat flags.
- Equipment: weapon lines (damage, Acc, range, RoF, shots, ST, Bulk, Rcl, reach, parry, skill and defaults), DR by location, Weak Points, servo ST, Basic Move and HP bonuses, `block` attributes (shields).
- `data/sim/loadouts.yaml`: default kit per template.
- `data/sim/weapons.yaml`: per weapon line, facts the stat line doesn't carry: `malf`, `overheat` (damage to the firer on a critical failure), `blast` (radius in yards for non-`ex` area weapons), `cone` (yards), `db` (shield Defense Bonus).
- `data/sim/powers.yaml`: psychic powers per template.

## Not modelled

Vehicles, stealth detection beyond an ambush option, psychic powers other than attacks and defences, morale beyond the half and quarter Fright Checks (the full Fright Check table, B360, is reduced to rout or stun).

## The tabletop replay

The simulator page replays the first battle of a run on a canvas map. Its data comes from the sample run only: each second's frame holds every model's hex, facing, health (share of HP, or the worst wound box under Fractional Health), the hexes it walked, condition bits (stunned, prone, kneeling, held, holding, All-Out Defense, All-Out Attack, aiming, pinned, waiting), shield share and aim target; each second's events hold shots and blows (who, whom, skill, hits), blasts, hits (injury and location, or none when armour stopped it), defences, shield absorptions and casualties. The map texture is generated from the layout (no image files), the same in light and dark themes.
